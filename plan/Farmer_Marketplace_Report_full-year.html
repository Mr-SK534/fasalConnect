Team Zenith · Business Model

Farmer Marketplace
Scaled 100-Farmer Model

Full-year economics across four Indian tomato seasons — redesigned fee structure for sustainable platform growth.

Scenario: 100 farmers × 25 tons of tomatoes annually = 25,00,000 kg total produce, sold across all four real Indian tomato seasons using a revised fee structure for healthier platform economics.
1

What Changed From the 6-Farmer Model

ChangeOld (6-farmer)New (this model)Reason
Logistics fee₹3/kg, 100% pass-through — zero platform revenue₹2.5/kg charged to buyer: ₹2 to partner + ₹0.50 platform marginStandard logistics-aggregator model (Uber/Ola) — platform earns for coordinating, not just facilitating
Fixed costs₹1,20,000/year (rough guess)₹90,000/yearMore realistic estimate based on low-cost hosting tiers (Railway/Render, minimal LLM API usage)
Commission8% of farmer price8% of farmer price — kept the sameAlready reasonable vs traditional ~65% take; logistics margin was the bigger lever
Scale6 farmers, 150 tons/year100 farmers, 2,500 tons/yearDemonstrates the model where fixed costs stop dominating economics
Every consumer-facing price was verified season-by-season to confirm it stays below the traditional retail price in every single season — not just on average. The ₹2.50/kg logistics charge was deliberately kept low to fix a gap in an earlier draft where the platform was briefly more expensive during the glut season.

Why This Model Is a Stronger Pitch

📈

Net profit went from ₹27,300 (6 farmers) to ₹36,40,000 (100 farmers) — not a 16.7× increase (what pure volume scaling predicts) but far larger, because fixed costs (₹90,000) barely grew while revenue grew ~16×. This is the actual SaaS/marketplace dynamic — a real economic argument for scale, not an inflated number.

🛒

Every season now shows the consumer paying less than the traditional chain — a gap in the earlier revision has been fixed by right-sizing the logistics fee. This is a materially stronger, more defensible claim: "our platform is cheaper for consumers in every season, not just on average."

🌾

The farmer earnings gain (+131%) is consistent across every season — because it derives entirely from cutting out the mandi-commission-agent layer, which happens at a constant rate regardless of season.

9

Caveats

  • Seasons 1 and 4 use documented historical price patterns, not confirmed 2026 readings (data not available/researched for those months).
  • The 33% farmer-share and 1.31× retail-markup ratios are national averages, not tomato-and-season-specific figures.
  • Platform cost line items (cloud, ops, marketing) remain team estimates — be ready to defend these as "reasonable assumptions" rather than verified data if a judge probes.
  • This model assumes 100% sell-through at listed prices and even distribution across seasons — real farmer behavior and real demand will vary, which is precisely the gap the demand-forecasting module is designed to help close.
